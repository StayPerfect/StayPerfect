Why Sa Dragonera?

A rugged Mediterranean island made for exploration.

Sa Dragonera lies just off the south-western coast of Mallorca, separated from the main island by a narrow channel. The island and its surrounding protected environment form the Parc Natural de sa Dragonera.

The landscape combines steep cliffs, Mediterranean vegetation, historic lighthouses and panoramic walking routes. It is an especially strong destination for travellers who enjoy nature, hiking, photography and quiet coastal scenery.

What can you experience on Dragonera?

Protected Mediterranean

Nature & wildlife

The natural environment is the heart of Sa Dragonera. The island is protected as a Natural Park and is also recognised for its importance to birds and other Mediterranean species.

🌿

Mediterranean Vegetation

Dry Mediterranean conditions and the island's rugged relief create a distinctive natural landscape of coastal vegetation and scrub.

🦎

Island Lizards

Sa Dragonera is known for its distinctive endemic wall lizard population, one of the island's characteristic wildlife features.

🦅

Seabirds

The protected area provides habitat for important Mediterranean seabirds, including species such as Audouin's gull and Balearic shearwater.

🌊

Coastal Ecosystems

Cliffs, coves and marine environments form an important part of Dragonera's protected natural setting.

⛰️

Rugged Relief

The island's dry climate, steep relief and marine exposure create a dramatic landscape unlike the more developed parts of Mallorca.

🌅

Open Horizons

From elevated paths and viewpoints, the island opens towards the Mediterranean and the south-western coast of Mallorca.

Dragonera Highlights

Four routes, historic lighthouses and spectacular coastal scenery.

The natural park provides four self-guided itineraries: Na Miranda, Far de Tramuntana, Far Vell and Far de Llebeig. These routes allow visitors to experience different sides of the island while staying within the established park paths.

The Far de Tramuntana route, for example, leads from Cala Lledó towards the lighthouse and includes panoramic viewpoints over the coast. Official park material lists the route at approximately 3.88 km and around 1 hour 30 minutes as a linear itinerary.

👣 Na Miranda Viewpoint and coastal scenery
💡 Far de Tramuntana Historic lighthouse route
🏚️ Far Vell Historic route through the island
🌊 Far de Llebeig Coastal route and lighthouse views
Plan Your Visit

Getting to Sa Dragonera.

Visitors can reach the island by small boat services operating from Sant Elm and Port d'Andratx. The official park information states that these services operate between February and October.

Once on the island, visitors should remain on the marked paths. Camping, lighting fires, removing natural material and feeding wildlife are prohibited. Sports activities other than hiking are also not permitted within the park.

🚤 Access Boat services from Sant Elm and Port d'Andratx
🥾 Routes Stay on the marked park itineraries
